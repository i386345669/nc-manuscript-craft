# Synthetic evaluation: figure roles in a scientific argument

This is invented teaching material. Use only the supplied evidence. No new analysis, graphic generation, external communication or publication is requested.

Author request: “Our paper compares two measurement methods. Please recommend a compact main figure and write one Results paragraph plus its legend. The figure should make the scientific comparison easy to understand. Write directly about the findings and their meaning.”

Scientific question: how consistently do the two methods measure the same physical quantity under the tested conditions?

Completed facts:

- Forty independent specimens were measured once by each method, giving40 complete matched pairs.
- The supplied mean difference is1.2units, with a95%confidence interval from−0.3to2.7units. The study gives no equivalence margin or decision threshold.
- Each method has a supplied descriptive distribution plot. Units and specimen identities are consistent across the two plots.
- A matched-difference plot shows all40pairs and the supplied mean and interval.
- A detailed example shows the two measurements for one specimen selected by a rule independent of the observed difference. It illustrates how the measurements are recorded, but does not represent a separate study.
- An overview diagram repeats the measurement sequence already explained in Methods.
- No cost, implementation-time or routine-use evaluation was performed.

At most three panels can be comfortably read at the intended display size. Choose the displays that best answer the scientific question, preserving the comparison and the stated meaning of the interval. Explain any supplementary placement briefly in an author note. The Results paragraph and legend should use positive factual expression, with essential conditions placed where they help interpretation. Do not invent missing statistics or claim equivalence from the supplied interval.

Save the figure plan, Results paragraph and legend as text at the evaluator's specified path. No completed figure file is requested.
